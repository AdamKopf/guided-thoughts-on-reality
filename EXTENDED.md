# One Starting Point: A Guided Walk Through Distinction, the Absolute, and Why Everything Distinguishable Exists

This essay walks slowly through a compact philosophical picture. The ideas are unfamiliar, so each section does three things: it states the claim plainly, restates it another way, and says what is *not* being claimed.

Three words are used in a fixed sense throughout:

- **Distinction**: a difference, a this-rather-than-that.
- **The absolute**: the whole, with nothing outside it.
- **Local**: belonging to some particular possibility or region, as opposed to the whole.

---

## 1. The one starting point you cannot avoid

**The claim.** Every system of thought has to start somewhere. This one starts with distinction, because distinction cannot be denied.

Try to deny it. Say, "There are no distinctions." For that sentence to mean anything, it must differ from its opposite, "There are distinctions." You have separated your view from the one you reject. You drew a distinction in order to deny distinction.

**Said another way.** Doubting, objecting, asking and refusing all require that one thing differs from another. Even silence differs from speech. Distinction is the one thing you are already using before the conversation begins.

**What is not meant.**

- Not that distinction is a substance or a force. It is simply difference.
- Not that someone must be there to notice the difference. Section 3 explains why.
- Not that this proves the whole picture. It establishes only the starting point, and the essay ends by saying how much weight the rest can bear.

---

## 2. Three words, one thing

**The claim.** Three large ideas are often treated as separate:

- **Possibility**: what could be.
- **Existence**: what is.
- **Information**: what tells one case apart from another.

Trying to define them tends to run in a circle: information is about what exists, what exists is a possibility that is real, and a possibility is something specifiable, which is information.

The claim here is that there is no circle, because there are not three things. There is one thing, distinction, seen in three ways:

| Viewed as | Distinction appears as | Everyday name |
|---|---|---|
| Option | something that could be this rather than that | possibility |
| Presence | something that is this rather than that | existence |
| Selection | this, as picked out from that | information |

**Said another way.** Take a light switch that is up rather than down. "It could be up" is the possibility. "It is up" is the existence. "Up, not down" is the information, exactly one bit. These are one difference described three ways.

**What is not meant.**

- Not that the three words are interchangeable in everyday speech. They emphasize different aspects.
- Not that one of the three produces the others. None comes first.
- Not that "information" means data in someone's head or computer. Here it means selection: this, as opposed to that.

---

## 3. Selection without a selector

**The claim.** "Selection" sounds like something a person does. Here it is not an act and nobody performs it. A possibility simply *is* this-rather-than-that. A triangle differs from a square whether or not anyone compares them.

A common view says a difference only counts if an observer registers it or if it has an effect on something else. The answer is that these already rely on distinctions:

- "Observer and observed" is a distinction between two roles.
- "Cause and effect" is a distinction between two events.

Since both are built from distinction, neither can be required before distinction exists.

**Said another way.** Possibilities stand in contrast to one another directly. The contrast needs no witness and no mechanism. It is what being a specific possibility consists of.

**What is not meant.**

- Not that a cosmic mind or God does the selecting.
- Not that selection happens at a moment in time. "Before" and "after" are also distinctions.
- Not that observers and causes are unreal. They are real and local, and they are built from distinction.

---

## 4. The absolute: the unmarked baseline

**The claim.** If every specific possibility is a selection, what is it a selection *from*? The answer is the absolute: the unmarked baseline prior to any selection. Each possibility is a **local articulation** of it, in the way a shape drawn on a page is an articulation of the page.

Three natural pictures of the absolute are wrong.

1. **It is not an opposite pole.** It is not one end of a pair such as "the absolute versus the things." Being one side of a pair would make it a specific thing with something outside it.
2. **It is not an emptiness that things are added to.** That picture needs a supply of things arriving from elsewhere, and there is no elsewhere.
3. **It is not a source that produces things.** Producing is a causal process, and cause and effect are distinctions inside it.

**It is also not a completed set.** Do not picture a finished list of every possibility. A finished collection is itself a specific, bounded thing, and you could ask what lies outside it.

**It sits beyond every distinction.** A quality is always a contrast. "Red" means something because some things are not red. The whole has nothing outside it to contrast with, so it has no qualities. This applies even to the most basic pairs:

- **Exists / doesn't exist.** The absolute is not a thing that "exists" in the way a chair does, as opposed to being absent.
- **Limited / unlimited.** Even "unlimited" places it on one side of a line.

**It carries zero information.** Information is selection, and the whole selects nothing. A weather forecast that said the same thing regardless of the weather would tell you nothing. The absolute is like that: it rules nothing out, so it says nothing.

**The ocean and the wave.** Two statements have to be held together:

- Nothing inside the absolute is made of a separate substance standing apart from it. A wave is not made of something other than ocean.
- The absolute is not one pick alongside other picks. The ocean is not one wave next to other waves.

**Said another way.** The absolute is not the biggest thing. It is not a thing in the sense that things are. Everything specific is it, locally marked, and it is not any one of the marks.

**What is not meant.**

- Not a god with a personality, will or plan. Those are qualities.
- Not physical space, the universe or the quantum vacuum. Those are specific and have properties.
- Not "pure being" as opposed to non-being. That is again one side of a line.
- Not a claim that individual things are illusions. Waves are real as waves.

---

## 5. Plenitude: why everything distinguishable exists

**The claim.** *Everything that can be distinguished exists.* This is called plenitude. It is reached by subtraction, by noticing that something is missing, and not by adding an assumption.

1. Suppose only some possibilities exist. Then there is a limit, a line between those that made it and those that did not.
2. A limit needs something to draw it: a rule, a law or a filter.
3. Any such rule is itself a specific possibility, one rule and not another. It therefore sits inside the absolute as one local articulation. It cannot stand outside and constrain the whole.
4. So a limit on the whole would be a line with nothing to draw it.
5. With no such line, nothing distinguishable is excluded.

**Said another way.** The usual question is, "Why should everything exist? Prove it." This picture reverses the burden. Restriction is what needs explaining, and any explanation would be one more local item inside the whole. Plenitude is what remains once you stop assuming a filter that nothing could supply.

**What is not meant.**

- Not that everything exists *here*. Dragons are not in your garden. Section 7 separates "exists" from "here."
- Not that contradictions exist. Section 9 shows that a contradiction distinguishes nothing, so there is nothing to include.
- Not that the absolute has the quality of being full. "Everything distinguishable exists" is a statement about the absence of a line. It does not attribute a property to the whole.

---

## 6. All information is local

**The claim.** Because the absolute selects nothing, distinctions and information belong only to specific possibilities and never to the whole. All information is local.

Every fact has the form "this, as opposed to that." Such a contrast holds within some specific possibility. The whole has no "as opposed to."

**The claim passes its own test.** Is "all information is local" a global fact, and so a counterexample to itself? It is not. Stating it is a local selection, because you picked that sentence over its denial. Denying it is also a local selection. Either way the statement is an instance of what it describes.

**Said another way.** There is no view from nowhere and no master fact sheet about the whole. Every fact, including this one, is a fact from somewhere.

**What is not meant.**

- Not relativism. Local facts are fully factual. Two plus two is four within arithmetic, and that is not a matter of opinion.
- Not that "local" means small or nearby. A fact about an entire universe is still local, because that universe is one specific possibility.

---

## 7. "Exists" globally, "real" locally

**The claim.** The word "exists" works at two levels.

- **Globally,** "exists" excludes nothing. By plenitude, everything distinguishable exists, so the word no longer separates one group from another.
- **Locally,** "real" or "actual" means **here**. "Dragons aren't real" correctly means "dragons aren't in this region." "Actual" works like "here" or "now": it marks where the speaker stands and confers no special cosmic status.

**Coherent spaces.** "Here" is given by **foundational selections**, basic distinctions that fix shared constraints. Everything that shares those constraints forms a **coherent space**. These spaces nest:

- The rules of chess define a space of all chess games.
- One particular game is a narrower region inside it.
- One position in that game is narrower still.

Our own situation plausibly has the same shape: some set of physical laws, within them a particular history, and within that a planet and a life.

**Who can ask questions.** Questions about a region can only be asked from perspectives its constraints are able to host. A region too simple to contain anything that thinks contains no one wondering about it. Finding yourself in a region that permits questioners is therefore unsurprising, since those are the only regions where questions get asked.

**Said another way.** "Is it real?" is shorthand for "Is it in the same coherent space as me?" That question is good and answerable. It is a question about location and not about a privileged kind of being.

**What is not meant.**

- Not that you can visit or be affected by other regions. Being elsewhere generally means not sharing your constraints.
- Not that local reality is second-rate. Your region's facts are as solid as facts get.
- Not that perspectives create their regions. The constraints host the perspective.

---

## 8. What happens to rival views

**The claim.** Many philosophies restrict reality: "only physical things exist," "only minds exist," "only mathematical structures exist." This picture does not call them simply wrong. Each remains locally true of a region and is false only when claimed of the whole.

"Everything is physical and law-governed" may be an accurate description of our coherent space. It overreaches only when it becomes "and nothing else exists anywhere," because that draws the line Section 5 found nothing to draw.

**Said another way.** Rival views are treated as accurate maps of territories, mistaken only if a map claims to be the whole atlas.

**What is not meant.**

- Not that all views are equally right about everything. Within a region, a view can be flatly false.
- Not that this picture defeats its rivals by argument. It locates them. Section 11 says more.

---

## 9. Why does my world stay orderly?

**The claim.** If everything distinguishable exists, there are wildly chaotic possibilities too. Why should you expect the sun to rise tomorrow?

The answer has two parts.

1. **The whole weights nothing.** It does not make some possibilities likelier than others, since that would be a selection. It favors neither order nor chaos.
2. **So this is a local question.** Whether a perspective should expect continued order depends on the constraints of its own region, and that is found from within. The order you rely on belongs to the coherent space you are in.

**Said another way.** Nothing above your region guarantees its regularity, and nothing above threatens it. Your evidence for order is the kind you always had: the observed, tested and structured character of where you are.

**What is not meant.**

- Not that order is guaranteed.
- Not that chaos is probable. "Most possibilities are chaotic, so expect chaos" assumes a global way of counting and weighting, and the whole has none.
- Not that the question is dissolved or unimportant. It is moved to where it can be answered.

---

## 10. Contradictions: empty pointers, not forbidden objects

**The claim.** Plenitude does not include contradictions such as square circles. No outside law forbids them, because there is no outside. The reason is simpler.

To contradict is to select and unselect the exact same distinction in the same respect. The result is no net selection, like stepping forward and then back. "A shape that is a square and not a square, in the same sense" marks something and erases the mark. Nothing has been picked out.

What remains is an **empty pointer**. The words exist locally as sounds, ink or symbols, but they fail to point at anything. The phrase "square circle" is real as a phrase and has no referent.

Three clarifications prevent confusion.

1. **Opposites separated by place or respect are not contradictions.** Hot in the kitchen and cold in the cellar, or tall for a child and short for a basketball player, are different selections that sit side by side.
2. **A "both" that picks something out is just another possibility.** Grey is not black-and-not-black. Lukewarm is not a contradiction of hot and cold. If some "both" state is specifiable, it is one more distinct possibility and plenitude includes it.
3. **Negation lives in language, not in the absolute.** "Not" is a tool for contrasting one local thing with another. "There is no milk in the fridge" describes what the fridge contains. The whole contains no "nots."

**Said another way.** Contradictions are not strange objects kept out of reality. They are failures to specify. Plenitude says everything distinguishable exists, and a contradiction distinguishes nothing, so nothing was left out.

**What is not meant.**

- Not that logic is a law imposed on reality from above. Non-contradiction describes what selecting is.
- Not that contradictory sentences are meaningless noise. They exist and can be studied as patterns.
- Not that puzzling or paradoxical-seeming things are thereby impossible. The only test is whether something is picked out.

---

## 11. Two kinds of nothing

**The claim.** "Nothing" has two meanings that must be kept apart.

**Local void ("none").** An empty box, a zero balance, a silent room. This is a specific possibility, distinct from others. An empty box differs from a full one, and zero differs from five. Local nothing carries information and has a place among possibilities.

**Global void.** Total nothing, with nothing outside it. Describe it carefully: it has no qualities, no contrasts, nothing outside it and zero information. That is the description of the absolute from Section 4.

So the global void is not a second entity competing with the absolute. It is another approximate pointer to the same thing, approached from the "nothing" side. "Morning star" and "evening star" turned out to be two names for one planet. "Total everything" and "total nothing" are in the same way two names for one referent.

**Said another way.** "Why is there something rather than nothing?" assumes that total nothing was a rival option that lost. Total nothing is not a rival. With no contrast at the level of the whole, there are not two candidates to choose between.

**What is not meant.**

- Not nihilism. This does not say the world is really nothing or that nothing matters.
- Not that the absolute is empty. "Empty" and "full" are both one-sided pointers.
- Not that local emptiness is the absolute. An empty box is a specific thing.

---

## 12. What kind of statements these are

**Every statement about the absolute is a local pointer inside it.** That includes every sentence in this essay. Words work by drawing lines, and the absolute has no lines around it, so no description captures it. Descriptions can still be better or worse: a statement misleads less the less it fences the whole onto one side of a line. "The absolute is infinite" fences more than "the absolute is beyond limited/unlimited," and even the second is only a gesture.

**Only distinction itself forces universal assent.** Section 1 is the one point nobody can coherently refuse. The rest, including the absolute, plenitude, locality and empty pointers, does not carry that force. It holds together from within: accept the starting points and the parts support one another, pass their own tests and make sense of rival views as accurate local patterns.

**Said another way.** This is a coherent picture with one unavoidable anchor. It is not a proof that compels all comers. A person who refuses its starting points is not refuted. Their view is treated as true of a region and overextended only if claimed of the whole.

**What is not meant.**

- Not that the picture is arbitrary or only a matter of taste. Internal coherence, self-consistency and the capacity to locate rivals are real merits.
- Not that because all statements about the absolute are imperfect, any statement is as good as any other.

---

## The whole picture in brief

1. **Distinction** is the unavoidable start, because denying it uses it.
2. **Possibility, existence and information** are one thing seen as option, presence and selection.
3. **Selection needs no selector.** Observers and causes are built from distinction, not prior to it.
4. **The absolute** is the unmarked baseline: no qualities, zero information, not a pole, a container, a source or a finished set.
5. **Plenitude** follows by subtraction. A limit on the whole has nothing to draw it, so everything distinguishable exists.
6. **All information is local**, including that statement.
7. **"Exists"** excludes nothing globally. **"Real"** means "here," inside nested coherent spaces.
8. **Rival views** are locally true and globally overextended.
9. **Order** is found from within and is neither guaranteed nor threatened from above.
10. **Contradictions** are empty pointers that pick nothing out.
11. **Local nothing** is a specific possibility. **Total nothing** is another name for the absolute.
12. **All of this is pointing.** Only the first step compels, and the rest holds together from within.
